How tiling works for layer 1

The logical matmul z₁ = W₁ · x + b₁ has shape (96, 40) · (40,) + (96,) = (96,). The tensor core only computes 4×4 tiles at a time, so the full matmul is decomposed into small per-tile operations and accumulated. At the same time, W₁'s 3,840 weights are distributed across 4 DMEM banks based on their flat index, meaning a single tile may need to pull from multiple banks in one cycle. This visualizer shows every tile, every DMEM read, and the accumulator state as the hardware works through layer 1.

Output tiling — 96 output rows split into 24 strips of 4 rows.
Inner-dim tiling — 40 input elements split into 10 tiles of 4.
Total tensor-core ops — 24 strips × 10 inner tiles = 240 WMMA instructions, each doing 16 BF16 multiplies + 16 BF16 adds → a running 4-element accumulator.

DMEM banks · reads for current tile

4 × 2048 × 16-bit BF16
Each tile needs 16 weight reads (4×4 from W) + 4 input reads (x slice). The 4 banks are independently addressable — reads to different banks happen in parallel in a single cycle. When a tile row straddles banks (near boundary flat-index 2,048), the hardware reads from two banks at once.
